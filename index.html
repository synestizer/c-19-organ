C-19: The inflatable organ

An eight-metre globe becomes an organ you can enter. Thirty-six outward spikes guide air through 3D-printed flutes and organ pipes, each shaped to sound at a different pitch.

Walk around the structure and the sound shifts with your angle of approach. Step inside and its permeable skin turns the globe into an echo room: a place to listen, gather and inhabit sound together.

C-19 grew from artist Kaspar König’s response to the aftermath of COVID-19. Made with Jope Schneider, the inflatable was presented with EAR — Environmental Artistic Research — in Switzerland in 2023, then travelled to Hong Kong.

Designed for indoor and outdoor settings, C-19 can welcome 10 to 15 people inside at a time. Concerts, projections and performances can unfold within it. Outdoor presentations depend on the weather: rain can make the structure too heavy to inflate.

The organ’s sound is omnipresent, not overwhelming: its level does not exceed 80 dBA. Every visit offers a different point of listening.
